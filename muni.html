MUNI

Digital signage for San Francisco's transit riders

Role
Sole UI/UX Designer, end to end
Client
Digital signage hardware company, San Francisco
Timeline
Three months in design, live since 2022
Delivery
Fully remote
  1. 01Problem
  2. 02Approach
  3. 03Solution
  4. 04Results
01Problem

Transit information every rider can read, from the curb

The client, a San Francisco company specializing in hardware for digital signage, needed an interface for screens at bus stops across the city. The deadline was three months, and the whole project ran remotely.

A bus stop screen is public infrastructure. It has to be legible at street distance, stay readable for riders with low vision or color vision deficiency, and win approval from the city as well as the client.

02Approach

Settle every constraint before drawing a screen

Before sketching a single screen, I asked the client the questions that would shape everything else.

  • Which information do we need to display?
  • What are the government's requirements?
  • Does it need to be ADA compliant?
  • Where will it run, and on which hardware?

ADA requirements

  1. 01

    Font size & scale

    I sized the type to stay legible at street distance, to the Americans with Disabilities Act's accessible design standards.

  2. 02

    Contrast ratios

    I checked every pairing of text and background against ADA contrast requirements before it shipped.

  3. 03

    A palette for colorblind riders

    I chose route colors and status states to stay distinguishable for riders with color vision deficiency.

From constraints to rollout

  1. 01

    Understanding the constraints

    Gathered the government, hardware, and accessibility requirements the interface would have to satisfy before any screen was drawn.

  2. 02

    Working the layout by hand

    Explored arrangements for routes, arrival times, and maps at low fidelity, before committing to any single direction.

  3. 03

    Building the real UI

    Turned the strongest sketches into detailed, polished screens, applying type, contrast, and color that met every ADA requirement.

  4. 04

    Client and government approval

    Reviewed the mockups with both the client and the local government to confirm the design met every accessibility standard.

  5. 05

    From mockup to build

    Handed off specs and assets, working closely through implementation to keep the shipped screens faithful to the design.

  6. 06

    Approved and deployed

    A final round of approval from the client and the city cleared the interface for rollout across the network.

03Solution

An arrivals board and a route map, built to ADA standards

I sized the type for street distance, checked the contrast of every pairing, and chose a palette that stays distinguishable for colorblind riders.

04Results

Approved by the city, and running since 2022

The screens run in various locations across the city, bus stops among them.

800+ screens deployed, approved by the client and the City of SF, operational since 2022.
